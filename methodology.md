## Methodology

### Approach

The objective was to identify companies that match DeepThought’s “Federer” profile — technically driven manufacturers with growth intent and in-house capability building.

The focus was on Indian specialty manufacturing sectors such as pharmaceuticals, biotech, and specialty chemicals.

---

### Sourcing Process

An initial pool of companies was created using:

- Targeted Google searches (e.g., API manufacturers, specialty chemicals)
- Company websites and industry listings
- Similar-company discovery from known firms

AI tools (ChatGPT, Brave AI, Google AI) were used as **assistive tools** to:
- expand the initial list of companies
- estimate missing data such as revenue bands
- suggest comparable companies

However, **AI outputs were not used directly**.

Each company was manually verified by:
- visiting the official website
- checking whether it is a manufacturer (not a service company)
- validating product type and positioning

---

### Filtering Logic

Each company was evaluated using DeepThought’s six criteria:

- C1: Manufacturer (mandatory filter)
- C2: India-based operations
- C3: Differentiation (R&D, niche products, certifications)
- C4: Technical leadership (where available)
- C5: Sector tailwinds
- C6: Growth signals (exports, hiring, expansion)

Companies were intentionally categorized into:
- Strong fit
- Moderate fit
- Reject / non-fit

to demonstrate filtering ability.

---

### Handling AI Hallucination

To control AI hallucination:

- AI outputs were treated as **suggestions, not facts**
- Every company was cross-checked manually via its website
- Any uncertain information (e.g., revenue, leadership) was either:
  - approximated conservatively, or
  - explicitly marked as “not publicly disclosed”

In multiple cases, AI-suggested companies were rejected after manual validation when they were found to be:
- service companies (CROs, testing labs)
- too large (>₹500 Cr)
- not true manufacturers

---

### Challenges

- Limited public data for MSMEs
- Leadership details often not available
- Growth signals required indirect inference

---

### Assumptions

Where exact data was unavailable, reasonable assumptions were made based on:
- product complexity
- certifications
- export presence
- website activity

Revenue figures are approximate and represented in bands.

---

### Key Insight

The exercise highlighted that identifying the right company is primarily a filtering problem, not a discovery problem.

Many technically strong companies were excluded because they did not meet the Federer criteria (e.g., service firms or large corporations).
